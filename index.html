Introduction

Fair Hills of Ocean, Oh! recounts the arrival of the Boat PeopleBoat PeopleApproximately fifty million burlap-skinned, carrot-haired people who arrive simultaneously at every port city in the world aboard flimsy green boats. They carry no firearms, only daggers, bolos, and pikes, yet they take over three-quarters of the world’s port cities within thirty minutes. at every port in the world, the intrigues of the Matamoros Chapter of the Informal CommitteeInformal Committee for Advising Directors of Corporations and NationsA worldwide network of roughly one hundred chapters, loosely linked, whose motto is “Save the World first of All.” The Matamoros Chapter has thirteen members and is the novel’s central human organization., and a dolphin secret agent’s entanglement with the emperor of DomdanielDomdanielThe oceanic fortress-palace of the emperor of the World. The title alludes to a poem of longing for Ireland, James Clarence Mangan’s “The Fair Hills of Eire, O!” Lafferty’s title transfers that longing from Ireland to the Ocean.

The novel has sixteen chapters, organized around two geographic poles: the Dunes of Hell resort near Matamoros, Mexico, and the Domdaniel fortress in the Atlantic Ocean. Between these poles, the action moves through the world’s port cities, the ocean depths, and the invisible cliffs above the sea surface.

Chapters

I The Green Boats Land ▶

The novel opens with an invocation of “a great and deep oceanic mind behind all this” and a teasing reference to “the Foremost Secret.” The Boat People then appear simultaneously at every port in the world. Seven alphabetical port-city catalogs, from Aalborg to Cuxhaven, alternate with descriptions of the invaders and encounters with them.

Aalborg–Amapala . . . Amoy–Bangor . . . Barcelona–Blythe . . . Bombay–Calcutta . . . Callao–Cebu . . . Champerico–Chittagong . . . Churchill–Cuxhaven

Amapala: A gray-bearded water-walker announces they come “from the ocean” and explains the duty rotation: “only a few of us walk on the water, those of us who are required to serve a term of duty as water-walkers and wise men who know all the languages.” Bankok: A red-bearded man arrives on cockle-shell skis; the locals laugh; he warns that the last people who laughed were killed and stripped to skeletons: “and their skeletons still laughed at us.” Blyth: Citizens offer humanitarian aid; the Boat People reply “We do not come as refugees. We come as conquerors and occupiers!” A big-voiced man is catapulted ashore and demands a shore inspection. Calcutta: The Boat People kill and impale local people to get noticed, and the narrator reports the killings in a deadpan tone. Cebu: Nighttime arrival; the Boat People glow with one candle-power of light. They demand control of banks, newspapers, fishing, and government. Cuxhaven: Even the Fifth Reich’s “Lightning and Iron” military defenses crumble before the Boat People’s “corporate aura.”

The chapter estimates about 100,000 Boat People at each of roughly five hundred ports, or about fifty million in all. The Boat People have burlap-colored skin, carrot-orange hair, and a “Viking motif” that resembles the popular image of Vikings. One-tenth talk; the rest take turns. They carry daggers, bolos, and pikes. They took over three-quarters of the ports in thirty minutes, “almost bloodlessly,” by inducing fear. Their proposition: let us control your military and civil forces, deliver your brass to us “bound and with their thumbs broken” so we can play cards with them.

II The Double-Agent Arrives ▶

The Informal Committee for Advising Directors of Corporations and Nations has about a hundred loosely linked chapters in thirty countries. Its motto begins “Save the World first of All.” The Matamoros Chapter draws its thirteen members from nine countries and “the Jolly Rogers Floating Realm of Practicing Pirates.” Its members are Emmet Briny, Margret Kirkestol, Giobbe (Job) Balena, Nero Altomare, Denis Gouffre, Carol Meduna, Jorge Meduna, Esther Scarlet, Oscar Judgement, Vitus Rosario, Caterina Rosario, Abel Crispin, and Frank Voigt. Emmet is its informal head; Abel is its newest member.

They meet at Emmet’s Dunes of Hell resort, in barren desert between Matamoros and the Rio Grande. The resort’s central feature is the Devil’s Well, a huge natural spring connecting by deep channels to both the river and the Gulf of Mexico. Any fish can be caught from it. Sometimes the Devil Himself sticks his head out. The Devil’s-Head manifestation has a single malevolent eye and a murderous voice surrounded by lesser voices “like jackals yapping around the roaring of a lion.” A man once offered $100,000 to learn the trick; Emmet refused because he does not know how it works. The narrator insists: “It wasn’t an effect! it was real.”

The Committee debates the Boat People. Giobbe: they are physically formidable. Margret: she can barely keep from fighting their women. Emmet: he hired the biggest one as bouncer. Frank Voigt: they must be “short-time projections” that will “melt or evaporate” within thirty-six hours. Esther Scarlet asks why no one has checked the wood of their boats or the Boat People themselves. Oscar Judgement: “they lasso our minds . . . they lull and numb our apprehensions.” The members recognize that something is wrong but feel no urgency about it, and Oscar attributes this numbness to the Boat People’s influence.

Vitus Rosario asks how Emmet came to buy this impossible resort. The buildings look like movie sets: “colorfully old and sand-blasted,” but no locals remember them being built. Esther speculates that they are all “characters in Grandson of the Dunes of Hell right now.” Margret reports genuine ghosts on the premises. A high-pitched whistling sounds from the Devil’s Well. The double agent arrives “with a great booming, with echoing splash and crash,” surfaces, rests his chin on the stones, and grins “with a meter-wide grin.”

III Intrigue at the “Dunes of Hell” ▶

The double agent is Robert Dolphin Liffy, a bottle-nosed dolphin 2.985 meters long. He wears a garment resembling an Inverness cape and a translator and throat microphone strapped to his throat. He introduces himself to the three members who have not met him. Margret invites him to “rub auras with me!”; he promises Esther “a very special relationship with you on several different levels.” His ambition is the throne of Domdaniel: “If I make it I’ll not be the first fish-face to sit on that throne.”

Oscar Judgement demands intelligence. Robert dismisses the Boat People (“don’t worry about them”), explaining that his “vibes” had told him days ago the swarming would happen. He swam three and a half days to reach them, but dolphins experience “cubical time,” not the humans’ “flat time.” He knows the Boat People are connected to the “Spirits of the Deep” (his code name for the oceanic powers) and to the Domdaniel Empire, which is “much more mighty, civilized, rich, and populous than all the dry-land nations of the Earth put together.” No species with a cranial capacity under 1,150 cubic centimeters qualifies as a citizen.

Robert dives through the Devil’s Well and resurfaces in the main supper-and-show room, where about a third of the afternoon house is Boat People. They pay in apparently ancient gold: Carthaginian coins, Persian Dorics, Macedonian Philippei, and a twenty-stater piece bearing the name of the Bactrian king Eucratides. Robert turns on the laughing gas. A Siemens Report records slaughter in five of five hundred ports, one percent of the ports. Oscar, still uneasy about the satisfaction that seems to numb everyone’s judgment, presses Robert for a way to investigate Domdaniel.

Oscar declares: “Five of us, myself Oscar Judgement, Esther Scarlet, Carol and Jorge Meduna, and Margret Kirkestol are going to your Domdaniel within an hour.” Robert warns there is no way humans can travel there. Oscar insists: “You underestimate our technology, Dolphin.”

IV Prisoners in Domdaniel Dungeons ▶

Five humans (Oscar Judgement, Esther Scarlet, Carol Meduna, Jorge Meduna, and Margret Kirkestol) travel to Domdaniel by balloon with Robert Dolphin, who rides in a water tank and navigates by vibes. Robert plays chess with Jorge (losing) and announces his invention: a whistle-activated typewriter that “will type as fast as I can talk, and it does not make mistakes.” He plans to write “all my Secret Agent Case Histories on it, and also my Annals.” He wants the first book titled Phooo, “a cryptic title.”

News from the world suggests that the Boat People govern lightly even as they take over municipal and state governments. Anthropologists identify more than a hundred traits distinguishing them from other human populations. Robert calls the Ocean an encyclopedia and explains dolphin perceptions of mirages, the past, and the future. He offers a piece of dolphin lore: “You can cure warts with the spittle of a Braxley’s Swell-Fish.”

The balloon hovers over Domdaniel. Robert’s vibes go silent. Something rips the balloon “its entire eighty-five meter length” and jerks them down twelve kilometers through the air and another kilometer into the ocean. They land in the lower levels of Domdaniel, feet fastened to the floor. A Personage appears whose fiery face suggests a burning bush and cannot be viewed directly. “I am the Cat,” he says. “You are the mice.” The water rises to their chins.

Margret identifies the Personage as Carmody Overlark. He stops the rising water but keeps the prisoners’ feet fastened to the floor. A fish-faced servant takes Robert to his newly arrived whistle-activated typewriter. Esther protests, “Robert, you have betrayed us for a pot of message.” Emmet, heard through the communication link, recalls Carmody’s reported death. Margret defies Carmody’s threat of water snakes: “I am a naturalist who is not afraid of anything natural. But you are unnatural.” Carmody leaves the five one inch from drowning.

V And Breathe New World with Fishes’ Breath ▶

Back at the Dunes of Hell, the other eight Committee members monitor the world’s response: Emmet Briny, Giobbe Balena, Nero Altomare, Denis Gouffre, Vitus Rosario, Caterina Rosario, Abel Crispin, and Frank Voigt.

Giobbe delivers a media roundup: the Puerto Rico Liberation Movement, the “Save-the-Prong-Horn-Antelope” group, the “Class Room Teachers’ Syndicate,” the “National-County-Jail-Prisoners-Union,” the “Revolutionary-Vegetarian-Enforcement-League,” the “Death-to-Smokers-After-May-Ninth-Militant-Group,” the “Ad hoc Take-Over Junket,” the “Gaiety Unrestrained Gala League,” the “Urban Burglars Mutual Society,” and the “Popular Causes Punitive League” all claim credit for the landings. The “Get Em Out By Morning Riflemen’s Action League” fires off rounds. The “Citizens’ Defense League” is banned on arrival. The president is at “Sea Haven, the Third White House.” The prime minister is at “Ocean Eden, the Third Number Ten Downing Street.” The hit song is “First Fish Night.”

Caterina reads the Siemens Reports: synthetic throat-gills manufactured in Tampa can let a person breathe underwater. The boats’ short-lived material resembles Syntho-Form Wood. Nero reads Contemporary Trade Opportunities, which offers tips for the new markets. Caterina takes the paper to read advertisements for the Rapidity School of Languages and other courses whose knowledge is automatically forgotten. The paper then evaporates explosively in her face.

Emmet declares the arrival good for business: “The Fortune Wind is blowing.” Frank warns the gold is almost all counterfeit (“Oh yes, they’re right there with gold content!”). Emmet decides to mint his own: “I’ll mint gold under the inscription of King Tut himself. I’ll have my own picture on some of the twenty dollar gold pieces.” Giobbe asks: “Who will pay for it when it’s over with?” Emmet: “I don’t believe that anybody will have to pay for it. The fast trade isn’t ever going to be over with.”

A Boat Person interviewed by Siemens Reports says that his people are human, breathe underwater, and “get restless every few centuries and want to go somewhere and raise a ruckus.” He calls the ruler of Domdaniel the emperor of the World. He also describes the notion mill, formerly used to grind salt, and Ocean Rumor, a news service available on land as well as in the ocean.

At 10:15 p.m. of First Fish Night, the eight members receive their new throat-gills, installed in fifteen seconds under anesthesia. They descend through the Devil’s Well into the ocean. “The ‘green darkness’ of the night-time deeps is not total darkness.” They discover they can breathe underwater, swim powerfully, and even have an advantage over dolphins: “They didn’t need to surface to breathe. They didn’t need blow-holes.”

VI Heroic Age When We Were Young ▶

Back in Domdaniel. Margret Kirkestol confronts Carmody Overlark directly: “The most evil man in the world! . . . You are like an evil magnet attracting people to you.” Carmody takes it as a compliment. He warns the prisoners that if they fall asleep they will drown, and leaves to attend to “the notables.”

A fish-faced Tyrrhenian servitor appears: Secret Agent Twenty-Three, also Slave Twenty-Three and the emperor’s right hand. He is Robert’s close friend and a fellow participant in interspecies studies. He frees the prisoners’ feet and later installs throat-gills, talking to distract them from the pain. He distinguishes Tyrrhenian Fish from Tyrrhenian Men, who are closely related to the Boat People.

Twenty-Three attributes the synthetic throat-gills to Carmody. The manufacturer in Tampa is a deposed ruler who copied the apparatus already fitted to his own throat. Robert enters as Secret Agent Number One and ceremonially ordains Twenty-Three. His own authority comes from a thirty-five-dollar correspondence course and an additional ten-dollar payment permitting him to ordain others. He gives his memoirs the working title Phooo, or The Memoirs of a Secret Agent.

Twenty-Three says that Domdaniel is “more than a million years old” and has been ruled by the elites of several species. In ancient annals hidden in a cave, he found charms for toppling emperors and outwitting rivals. He believes the Boat People descend from Tyrrhenian Fish and Carmody’s para-human species. Their permanent settlements on land are on the Adriatic coast of Albania and the Bushire coast of Iran. Their boats, he says, are “propelled by the psychic hysteria and delirium of the Boat People.”

Carmody returns with the president of the United States, “Reddy Boy,” who proposes increasingly sadistic tortures. Carmody repeatedly defers them. He explains that the Matamoros Chapter attracted his attention by thinking and questioning outside the limits he had set for the world. After the two rulers leave, the prisoners declare their own heroic age. Margret exclaims, “I never knew before how sparkling it was to be heroic!”

VII Climbing Upward Through the Sky ▶

Oscar demands intelligence before escaping. Twenty-Three says that Carmody fathered two related races by “milting” fish roe many millennia ago, a story Oscar challenges. He evades questions about the plague and admits that his knowledge of the fortress has gaps. There is a chute through the walls, but he will not risk further help unless it advances his revolution.

Twenty-Three will not lead them through the ninth wall: invisible cliffs a thousand meters high in the air above the ocean, “as slick as ice,” with a “special aspect” too dangerous to mention. Robert Dolphin, as a dolphin, cannot climb them either. Margret persuades Robert to guide them through the first eight walls. “I believe that we can do things for you that this Personage cannot do.”

Robert guides the escape through eight walls. An opening ritual begins “This is the Iron Night, Night of the Dire Attempt” and puts the Sea Mastiffs guarding the first wall to sleep. Margret cuts the throats of all nine dogs. Oscar sustains the party with whistled music: “Terror in the Deep,” “Drowned Men’s Rag,” “When the Sea Gives Up Its Dead,” and “Nile Down Meander.” With their throat-gills, the humans can sing underwater.

Robert appears in another disguise, looking like a strange young dolphin. His Inverness cape conceals artificial throat-gills, which let a surface-dwelling dolphin survive in the depths. Margret and Robert repeatedly go apart during the escape. When he sulks and refuses to lead them through the eighth wall, their confrontation reveals that he is only five years old. Margret asks whether he has read What a Young Dolphin Boy Ought to Know. “There’s no such book, Margret,” he replies.

They break through the ocean’s surface half an hour before sunrise. Robert cannot climb the ninth wall’s invisible cliffs. He describes their sharp stalactites, then leaves by sky-shuttle on a mission he says will save mankind. Oscar distrusts his departure but leads the humans upward, whistling “Fair Ghost Invisible, I Long for Thee.”

VIII Disguises Cannot Long Conceal ▶

Robert Dolphin appears at World Medical Headquarters in New York on the morning of “Second Fish Day,” in five successive disguises: a weird hobbling tall man, an Arabian camel-driver in burnoose, a tall basketball player in New York Rounders sweats, a tall doctor with monocle speaking Barcelonian Spanish, and a cowboy twirling a rope. All are Robert, and none is taken seriously. Other administrators call the aquarium to report “a mad dolphin loose in midtown.”

Unable to get a hearing, Robert makes calls from a phone booth. Carmody cuts short his call to the president; Twenty-Three discusses the revolution over a separate radio-phone. Robert tells Emmet where he has left a disease sample and an easily replicated vaccine: locker 502 at the midtown YMCA in New York. He then relays a separate, hypnotically implanted invitation to Domdaniel, directing the eight members in Matamoros to a sand dune for transportation. Caterina reports that the impending plague ranks fifth among the morning’s wildest rumors.

Robert orders long-stemmed roses with “sharp-memory” thorns for a special delivery. He makes further calls and leaves the booth for a hotel pool. His cigarettes bear counterfeit Sullivan labels, and his rare lighter and elaborate nonchalance form part of his secret-agent performance.

At the pool, a Boat Person orders roast dolphin’s nose and identifies himself as Robert’s assigned tail. He says that the plague rumor is a manipulative tool and that his people intend to charge for the vaccine. Robert tries to lose him through three taxi journeys and three changes of disguise, then retreats into a basement. The tail is already there, still eating dolphin’s nose: “What took you so long?”

The chapter ends with the other eight Matamoros members receiving the decoded message at the Dunes of Hell. They walk to the coordinates. “Then—whoosh!—it came and got them.”

IX Remember Me with Thorns ▶

Oscar Judgement’s first contingent (Oscar, Esther, Carol, Jorge, and Margret) climbs the invisible cliffs of the ninth wall. The cliffs are crystalline, “the stairway to the next sphere.” The wind is exuberant. Sharp invisible stalactites wound them. They climb by feel, unable to see the surface. Twice, false narratives intrude, including purple prose about “bursting lungs and bloody-bruised hands,” which Oscar dismisses as planted fictions: “somebody was trying to fill them with defeat.”

A supersonic mini-car delivery arrives mid-climb: “One dozen long-stemmed roses for Miss Margret Kirkestol from one—ah—Robert Al-dolphin Liffy.” Margret, hanging by one hand on an invisible cliff, accepts them in her mouth. The card reads: “To my first love. Roses fade and die, but thorns are forever. Remember me with thorns.” The delivery pilot tells them they are climbing the outside of Domdaniel, not the inside: “A lot of people make that mistake.” He offers to send a sky-taxi.

They climb down and wait on a rock buttress of Domdaniel that protrudes above the ocean. Margret dribbles blood from the thorns: “It seems that young Robert Dolphin really does love me.”

Meanwhile, the other eight members (Emmet, Giobbe, Nero, Denis, Vitus, Caterina, Abel Crispin, and Frank Voigt) are snatched from the Dunes of Hell by the sky-shuttle. They crash down into Domdaniel “much faster than they would have descended in free fall.” Emmet roars: “The Emperor of Domdaniel will hear about this!” They land in the Masterpieces of Death room. Carmody addresses the absent Robert, praising his deception and wondering why he has not accompanied the prisoners. Twenty-Three claims that piranhas ate the first contingent. Carmody identifies Caterina: “She does not inflame me as much as the blonde Margret.” She calls him “the most evil man in the world!” Twenty-Three groans: “A damned replay!”

Carmody unveils the Masterpieces of Death, a twelve-sided gallery containing more than three thousand paintings and sculptures of deaths that occurred there. The great scene he lacks is the murder of Abel. The Devil painted it as an eyewitness, and Carmody longs to steal the painting from hell. A predecessor who stole it possessed it for seven seconds before suffering everlasting destruction.

X It’s Bloody but Is It Art? ▶

The chapter opens with a meditation on “two great and deep and oceanic minds duelling across an abyss.” Robert, in New York, smokes his Sullivan cigarette and lights it with his Limited Edition Lighter, performing the secret agent persona. But he is wracked with guilt: “looking at it one way, he was a shameful betrayer.” Agent Twenty-Three, “a very nice fellow (if you like fish-faces),” plots the palace revolution and recognizes that Robert must impersonate Carmody for the takeover to succeed.

In Domdaniel, Carmody continues the art tour with the eight new prisoners. The Masterpieces of Death are documentary: Alexander Hamilton did not die at Weehawken; deaf Beethoven did not die in 1827 but nine years later “with his head exploded”: “see the scorings and musical notations pouring out of his exploded head.” Raphael painted here for thirty-five years after his supposed death. Galileo was snatched by Carmody. Jeanne d’Arc was “grabbed off by a strange scooping device” from her burning and brought to Domdaniel. Thirteen popes visited; three did not return.

Denis calls Carmody “You devil, you bugger!” Carmody turns the insults into supposed divine titles by linking devil with deva and bugger with bog. He also describes the Sea People’s migrations and their remote burial grounds, known to Breton fishermen before Columbus.

Agent Twenty-Three, conducting the second part of the tour, plots with the slaves. They need Robert to impersonate Carmody. “Any really top Secret Agent could do it.” In New York, the Tail continues to shadow Robert, who is troubled by his conscience. The Tail brings him a daisy: “‘She loves me not’ means that you stay here and sulk. ‘She loves me’ means that you take a supersonic sky taxi back.” Robert plucks: “she loves me.” He orders a taxi.

The first contingent still waits on the rock buttress. Carmody, mid-tour, breaks off: “I must leave you here, briefly, to attend to private affairs.” Twenty-Three takes over.

XI The Twist and Turn of Fishy Mind ▶

The chapter opens with four epigraphs: “The Fair Hills of Eire, O!” (Mangan), “The hills of Ocean skip like lambs” (Old Dory Song Book), a “Nostradamus Dolphinus” prophecy (“When older King is overthrown / A fish-face shall possess the throne”), and a snippet from Palace Revolution, a Play in Nine Acts, by the Tyrrhenian Tyrant (Twenty-Three’s own work).

Twenty-Three conducts the eight prisoners through Domdaniel’s galleries. In the Golden Room, statues of mythological creatures are hammered so fine that some are translucent or float on static electricity. A dying third French horn player, an Euxine Harpy, lies among casualties of factional fighting. The “Poor Fishes’ Broadcast” announces Robert’s approach in verse.

Twenty-Three negotiates with the Committee. Emmet demands that the swarming and plague be recalled. Twenty-Three says a new ruler could countermand them, but questions why saving humanity matters so much. The eight agree to support his revolution; he appoints Emmet to his provisional council.

They visit the Oceanic Graphics Room and approach the Schism Room of Subliminal Art, then turn instead to the Beauty-To-Make-The-Blood-Pound-By Room. Carmody has placed living women among its images. The seven men are drawn away, leaving Caterina to replace Emmet on the provisional council. Twenty-one minutes are left before the revolution is due to begin; the plague will activate fifteen minutes later unless stopped.

The Poor Fishes’ Broadcast announces: “I’ll come to you, I’ll come to ye, / And be among you suddenly.”

XII Disguise Will Sit upon the Throne ▶

The first contingent (Oscar, Esther, Carol, Jorge, Margret) waits on the rock buttress. A sixth person appears “among them suddenly”: apparently Robert Dolphin, grand and imperially composed. Margret is smitten: “A thousand years from now I can still peal with delight ‘The Dolphin Emperor once sent me Roses!’” This Robert seems benevolent and all-knowing. He pays off a sky-taxi with a gold piece “the size of a quoit.” He tells them Domdaniel is now “the most benevolent place in the world.”

A second dolphin, grubby and awkward, then falls out of another sky-taxi. He resembles Robert but is less impressive in every respect, and he is bewildered: “Who are you? How come you look like me?” The five Committee members laugh cruelly. The grand Robert is compassionate. The grubby dolphin is left on the buttress: “I am not free to go while this thing is awry with the world.”

The grand Robert and the five descend into Domdaniel. The grubby dolphin is the real Robert, now alone. “I am out of my mind!” He realizes that the grand version is Carmody Overlark disguised as him. “But who is he then, if he makes a better me than I do?” He devises a counterstroke: “I’ll disguise myself as the Great Carmody, that’s what I’ll do. Two can play this game.”

Robert-as-Carmody descends to Domdaniel. Agent Twenty-Three recognizes him: “You do make a tolerable Tyrant Carmody, and he makes an excellent Robert Dolphin.” The Tyrrhenian Fish see through both disguises intuitively, but the ten thousand of them will say nothing. Robert-as-Carmody climbs the three hundred giant steps to the throne (awkwardly, since “dolphins are not great leapers except in the water”), orders the dragons-teeth soldiers to cover their eyes, and sits on the throne of Domdaniel.

On the throne, Robert experiences Carmody’s memories and pseudo-memories. They include service as Kar-im-Mod behind Akhnaton, the identity Khar-ibn-Mod, and a life as Haroun-al-Raschid. Robert presses the “Turn Swarm Off” and “Turn Plague Off” activators. As revolutionaries enter and kill the guards, he considers the human world probably saved. Still disguised as Carmody, he responds to the demand that he abdicate: “Oh, that’s easy. . . . I abdicate.”

XIII Interview with Ocean ▶

The Boat People are building new boats on every shore, making a show with axes and hammers “when land people were watching them; but that was all sham.” They build the boats already afloat, from plankton and algae. All fifty million are preparing to leave, less than twenty-four hours after arriving.

Most of the chapter is an interview between freelance writer Elroy Oldapple and Whitefin Slackwater, an informed Boat Person. Slackwater describes a population of about a billion in the oceans, Sea People living unnoticed on land, turtlenecks that hide throat-gills, and government by rotation among groups of nine adults. Oldapple lists conflicting explanations of Domdaniel; Slackwater says Carmody’s reign may end that day.

Further topics: property rights (rights to currents that move around), morality (“Oh sure. Are you?”), whale-hunting (“we drive them over cliffs”), monogamy (“one of those women of ours is sure enough”), gold coins (copied from old designs; “we might be accused of counterfeiting”), invisibility again (“We Sea People have always been the ‘Invisible People’”), Christianity (“we were the first Christians. Peter, Andrew, James, and John, all those Galilean commercial fishermen were Sea People”), the Ring of the Fisherman, clothing and tattooing (“we invented it”), boat construction (plankton and algae; “we eat them” afterward), arts (scrimshaw, live drama), dolphins (Robert as pop hero; “If you ever get down to the Mile Down Ride-In Cinema”), sports (shark rodeo), corruption (“We call it ‘clean corruption’”), tourism (“six weeks for six dollars”).

The plague: “Oh, that’s just been postponed. It’s just been scrubbed by imperial edict, push-button division.” The Tyrrhenian People and Tyrrhenian Fish: “just alike except that one group of them are people and the other group of them are fish.” They have been called “the Bureaucrats of the Ocean.” Carmody’s age: people live twenty times longer in the Ocean, so “the longest that Emperor Carmody could possibly have lived is about two thousand years.”

XIV And for a Snipe a Throne Was Lost ▶

Carmody, still disguised as Robert, enters at throne level with Margret and a large entourage. He changes back into his own form and rejects Robert’s abdication on his behalf. He orders a thousand Ataraxian guards to remove Robert. Robert sheds his material disguise, claims the authority of the occupied throne, and redirects the guards against Carmody.

Margret hacks the hand off the foremost guard with a short hooked knife. “Faint you not, Guardsman!” she orders, then commands him to pick up his severed hand. Robert swims in purgatorial waters, humiliated by having abandoned the throne for a cigarette. Ocean Rumor reports his despondency, and the Ocean Voice asks that someone direct him toward companionship. His lament draws on Isaiah, Job, Shakespeare’s Sonnet 129, and The Merchant of Venice.

Allusions & References

“The Fair Hills of Eire, O!”: James Clarence Mangan

The title recalls James Clarence Mangan’s poem “The Fair Hills of Eire, O!” Mangan adapted an Irish poem by Donnchadh Ruadh Mac Con Mara. Lafferty cites its title in the chapter 11 epigraphs, then supplies a comic oceanic counterpart from the Old Dory Song Book. In that verse the hills of Ireland become “the hills of Ocean.” Mangan’s text and its publication history appear in the University College Cork edition.

The Port-City Catalogs

The seven alphabetical port-city catalogs in chapter 1 run from Aalborg to Cuxhaven. They recall epic catalogs and litanies, while encounters at Amapala, Bankok, Blyth, Calcutta, Cebu, and Cuxhaven interrupt their rhythm. The lists establish the worldwide scale of the landings while the individual scenes show different local responses.

Domdaniel

Domdaniel is a traditional undersea haunt of evil magicians, used by Robert Southey in Thalaba the Destroyer and invoked by Thomas Carlyle in Sartor Resartus. Lafferty expands it into an imperial fortress with galleries, a throne room, dungeons, and machinery for directing events across the world. Twenty-Three calls it more than a million years old. Robert’s whistled name among its inhabitants is Wheecreoclessese.

Bunyan and St. John of the Cross

The title “Dark Night of Despond” combines language associated with St. John of the Cross’s Dark Night of the Soul and Bunyan’s Slough of Despond. The chapter explicitly recalls The Pilgrim’s Progress: “The name of the slough was Despond.” Robert is a living dolphin overcome by shame, visiting a settlement of purgatorial ghosts. Dan Asal, unlike Robert, is detained there for an unrepented killing.

Sullivan Cigarettes and Raffles

Sullivan cigarettes are a running motif: Robert’s fraudulently labeled packs, his elaborate nonchalance in lighting them, and the genuine cigarette that costs him the throne. Chapter 8 recalls the “palmy days of Raffles,” E. W. Hornung’s gentleman thief, who smoked Sullivans. In chapter 14, Twenty-Three says that manufacture ended a century earlier and the surviving stock passed to royalty. The allusion associates Robert’s secret-agent persona with Hornung’s gentleman thief.

The Ring of the Fisherman

The papal Anulus Piscatoris appears in the Slackwater interview (chap. 13) as an emblem of the Sea People’s connection to the Catholic tradition. “The non-oceanic Popes who also wear ‘The Ring of the Fisherman’ do it only by special grace and dispensation.” Slackwater claims Peter, Andrew, James, and John (“all those Galilean commercial fishermen”) were Sea People. Slackwater’s account takes the fisherman of the papal title literally.

Etymology: Devil / Deva / God

In chapter 10, Carmody answers Denis’s insults by claiming that devil means deva and that bugger is related to bog, making both into names for God. The wordplay is self-serving. The English words have different histories: devil comes through Latin from Greek diabolos, while bugger derives through French from a medieval Latin word for Bulgarian.

Pomp and Circumstance

Elgar’s march is played by “one thousand trumpets” in chapter 14 as Carmody Overlark departs through the Departures of Emperors door. A British imperial processional accompanies the exit of an ancient oceanic tyrant. Three fictional trumpet pieces mark the other events of the chapter: “Oh, Short Bright Reign” (for Robert’s abdication), “The Blade That Strikes by Night” (Twenty-Three’s election theme), and “Fish-Face, Fish-Face, How Far You Reach!” (the accession fanfare).

The Masterpieces of Death: Historical Figures

Carmody’s gallery (chaps. 9–10) relocates the deaths of historical figures to Domdaniel. Alexander Hamilton did not die at Weehawken; Beethoven survived 1827 by nine years before his head exploded; Raphael painted in the fortress for thirty-five years after his supposed death; and Galileo and Jeanne d’Arc were abducted there. Thirteen popes visited, and three did not return. The collection includes works attributed to artists from Praxiteles to Maxfield Parrish. Carmody says that each death depicted in the gallery took place there.

Carmody’s Historical Aliases

On the throne in chapter 12, Robert experiences Carmody’s memories of earlier identities, including Kar-im-Mod, a power behind Akhnaton; Khar-ibn-Mod, a Mameluke diplomat; and Haroun-al-Raschid. The convincing memories extend back roughly three thousand years, while earlier pseudo-memories lack the same detail. Carmody’s interventions on land have run alongside his rule in Domdaniel. Robert recognizes in them the same passion for secret agentry that drives his own career.

The Vikings and the Gypsies

The Boat People’s “Viking motif” in chapter 1 resembles the popular image of Vikings more than their historical appearance or behavior. In chapter 13, Slackwater claims a sea ancestry for the people the novel calls Gypsies: “Yes, originally they were Sea People, but now they’re mixed bloods.”

John’s Apocalypse: The Jewelry Whale

The final display of the Jewelry Whale invokes Revelation 21, where the New Jerusalem is adorned with precious stones. The narrator also recalls the toad’s “precious jewel in his head” from Shakespeare’s As You Like It (2.1.12–14). The echoes heighten the splendor of the treasure. The banquet at which it arrives is also the scene of theft and cannibalism.

Themes

Ocean as Sovereign Intelligence

The opening’s “great and deep oceanic mind” belongs first to Robert Dolphin, as chapter 3 makes clear. Chapter 10 extends the image to his contest with Carmody and raises the possibility of a third rival in Twenty-Three. The Ocean also has a collective presence: its rumors, broadcasts, and voice carry information and call for aid to Robert in his despondency.

Disguise and Identity

Robert relies on material disguises; Carmody can change his own form. When Carmody becomes a more convincing Robert than Robert himself, the dolphin’s confidence in his identity falters. Counterfeit cigarette labels and the Sea People’s unnoticed presence on land extend the pattern.

Imperium and Abdication

The emperor of the World rules from Domdaniel with push-button access to plague, swarming, tidal waves, and the end of the world. But he is “getting about ready to abdicate,” looking for a “Grand Final gesture.” The palace revolution transfers control of the activators from Carmody to Twenty-Three without dismantling them. The title of chapter 14, “And for a Snipe a Throne Was Lost,” treats the transfer as comedy: Robert gives up the throne for a cigarette.

The Notion Mill

A Boat Person describes Domdaniel’s notion mill in chapter 5. Once it ground salt for the oceans; now it supplies extravagant notions to the world. He contrasts these notions with the rational thought that supposedly preceded the mill’s new use. The passage leaves open how far the mill produced the Boat People’s own decision to swarm.

Purgatory and Conscience

Robert reaches St. Malo’s alive, overwhelmed by the public humiliation of losing the throne. Dan Asal is a dead man whose remorse stops short of repentance. Dan can write that he is sorry, but adds that he would commit the killing again. His repeated failure makes the distinction between shame and contrition visible. Robert’s recovery comes through conversation, invention, and renewed involvement with another dolphin; Dan stays at St. Malo’s until his will changes.

The Conquest as Comedy

The Boat People seize most ports with simple weapons and overwhelming psychological force. The apparent ease of the conquest makes it difficult for the Committee to feel the urgency it recognizes intellectually. The comic manner extends to the killings in Calcutta and the plague threat. The invaders’ clumsiness and sociability make their power difficult to take seriously.

The Slackwater–Oldapple Interview (Chapter 13)

Following a short account of the departing boats, chapter 13 presents an interview between Elroy Oldapple and Whitefin Slackwater. Its thirty-six exchanges cover oceanic population, government, property, religion, art, and relations with land-dwellers. Slackwater explains how the boats are built from plankton and algae, praises Robert’s celebrity, and reports that the plague has been postponed by imperial edict. His closing estimate of Carmody’s age, no more than about two thousand years, conflicts with Carmody’s own claims.

The “Masterpieces of Death” Gallery

A twelve-sided gallery in Domdaniel holds more than three thousand paintings and sculptures of deaths said to have occurred there. The tour in chapters 9–10 rewrites the lives and deaths of Galileo, Jeanne d’Arc, Hamilton, Beethoven, Raphael, and others. The scene Carmody most covets is absent: the Devil’s eyewitness painting of the murder of Abel. A previous emperor stole it from hell but possessed it for only seven seconds. Carmody exhibits the depicted deaths as works of art and as evidence of his power.

The Throne Activators

The emperor’s throne in Domdaniel carries buttons and legend plates, some “picked out in jewels” and others “written in the fine hand of Carmody Overlark that was like engraving”: “Turn swarm on,” “Turn swarm off,” “Turn plague on,” “Turn plague off,” “Activate end of the world,” “Awaken the slumbering giants,” “Have the ice age return,” “Let the flood return,” “Awaken the slumbering heroes,” “Let Catastrophe come,” “Let Doomsday come,” and “Make Domdaniel Invisible.” Robert pushes “Turn Swarm Off” and “Turn Plague Off” during the revolution: “Oh, it’ll probably save the human world, if they mean what I think they mean.”

The Coronation Trumpet Pieces

Four named pieces punctuate the abdications and election in chapter 14, before the coronation banquet:

The Fictional Secret-Agent Library

During the suicide-method dialogue in chapter 15, Robert and Dan Asal cite a series of fictional novels in which secret agents meet dramatic ends. The titles form a mock bibliography of the genre:

The Ultimate Secret of the Secret Agent, in which Horst Grell slashes his wrists. The Green Lady’s Ransom, in which Count Spencer-Fitzmorris cuts his throat. Dames of Death, in which Sid Secret dies. Bait for Vultures, in which Anthony Arbor dies. To the Bitter-Sweet End, in which Roderick Riddiechive dies (“Oh yes, Roderick had a lot of style”). Also referenced elsewhere: What a Young Dolphin Boy Ought to Know (a nonexistent book suggested by Margret), “The Day After Hell Got Full” (a “comic fable” by Furon Cassius), and the correspondence course “Now You Can Become a Secret Agent” (which cost Robert thirty-five dollars).

The Rose Card

Delivered by supersonic mini-car to Margret Kirkestol while she hangs by one hand on the invisible cliffs of the ninth wall (chapter 9), the card attached to Robert’s long-stemmed roses reads:

To my first love. Roses fade and die, but thorns are forever. Remember me with thorns, Robert Al-dolphin Liffy.

The thorns draw blood from Margret’s mouth as she accepts the roses while clinging to the cliff. The delivery pilot mentions that Carmody sends similar roses with the message “Roses and Death, from Carmody.” That is Carmody’s practice, distinct from Robert’s card to his first love.

The Boyhood Verse (Chapter 3)

Robert quotes G. K. Chesterton to describe his friendship with Twenty-Three:

He was, through boyhood’s storm and shower,
My best, my nearest friend.
We wore one hat, smoked one cigar,
One standing at each end.

The stanza opens “A Dedication: To E. C. B.” in Chesterton’s Greybeards at Play (1900). The comic verse suits Robert’s extravagant claim of friendship with Twenty-Three, who later outwits him.

Dan Asal’s Failed Apology

This recurring text keeps Dan Asal in purgatory. He must write “I’m sorry that I shot that guy” to be released. Each time he writes it, he adds a defiant postscript:

I’m sorry that I killed that guy—but I’d damnite do it again!

“Fire came down and destroyed that sheet of paper, but a new and unmarked sheet appeared in the place of it.” The pencil and paper always return. “It happens every time,” Dan says. “I spoil it every time.” The postscript cancels the apology; Dan can leave only when he writes the apology without it.

The Sullivan Exposé (Chapter 14)

Twenty-Three exposes the false Sullivan labels while Robert occupies the throne in chapter 14. He says manufacture ended a hundred years earlier and that the surviving stock went to royalty. He then offers a genuine cigarette, which Robert leaves the throne to retrieve, and demands the return of the rare lighter Robert had taken from him. The episode costs Robert the throne and shames him before the court.

Timeline Anomalies

Cubical time. Robert swam three and a half days to reach Matamoros, but the landings happened less than an hour and a half before his arrival. He invokes “cubical time”: “You have flat time. We have a touch of cubical time.” Whether this is a genuine supernatural property of dolphins or Lafferty’s deliberate flouting of causality is left unresolved.

Robert’s transit. Robert travels from mid-Atlantic (dawn) to New York City (early morning, same day). The text explains this by invoking Carmody’s sky-shuttle, which “sped him on his treacherous way.”

Landing time. Chapter 5 gives 10:00 p.m. EST as five hours after the landings. Chapter 1 also describes simultaneous arrivals at different local times, including night at Cebu. These descriptions do not establish a fully consistent worldwide clock.

Domdaniel time. Chapter 7 places the fortress three hours ahead of the Matamoros home base. Chapter 10 places its meridian thirty degrees west of Greenwich and imagines every minute originating there. The geography and personified time belong to the novel’s oceanic cosmology.

Glossary

Accelerated-modern
A neologism from Contemporary Trade Opportunities for the new economic era created by the Boat People’s arrival. “Modern (‘accelerated-modern’ is the new term) technology should be equal to the challenge.”
Agent Twenty-Three title
A Tyrrhenian Fish, Robert’s closest friend, and Carmody’s right hand. He serves as slave and secret agent, organizes the palace revolution, and succeeds Robert as the 289th emperor. Robert is Secret Agent Number One.
Algae number nine
A “come-all-ye solvent” used with plankton-lure on a framework of boards and driftwood. Microscopic organisms accrete to the framework and form the Boat People’s blue-gray-green boats. Slackwater describes the process in chapter 13.
Attar
A fragrant essential oil. Robert orders roses with “their odor attar-reinforced.” The English word comes through Persian from Arabic ʿiṭr, meaning perfume or essence.
Aureus
A Roman gold coin. Emmet Briny finds “quite a few aureus gold pieces circulated by the Emperor Marcus Aurelius” among the ancient coinage at the Dunes of Hell. The plural is aurei.
Bactrian
Of or relating to Bactria, an ancient region of Central Asia. A gold coin “issued by the Bactrian King Eucratides” appears in the ancient coinage at the Dunes of Hell. Eucratides I issued the largest gold coin of antiquity.
Beauty-To-Make-The-Blood-Pound-By Room
A gallery in Domdaniel containing images of human women and living women placed among them. Twenty-Three calls it “probably the least artworthy” of the major galleries. The seven men in the second Committee party are drawn away by its occupants, leaving Caterina to replace Emmet on the provisional council.
Boat People
Approximately fifty million burlap-skinned, carrot-haired arrivals who appear simultaneously at every port city in the world: about three hundred boats with three hundred people each at roughly five hundred ports. They carry daggers, bolos, and pikes but no firearms. One-tenth do the talking; the rest take turns. They glow with about one candle-power of light. Their boats are flimsy, three-decked, and appear to be made of still-living material. They are the Sea People who come ashore in the swarming.
Boat People Disease
A plague the emperor can activate via the throne activators in Domdaniel. Robert Dolphin warns World Medical Headquarters about it on “Second Fish Day.” Later “postponed”: “just been scrubbed by imperial edict, push-button division.”
Bureaucrats of the Ocean
Slackwater’s designation for the Tyrrhenian People and Tyrrhenian Fish, whom he describes as managerial races. Twenty-Three is a Tyrrhenian Fish.
Cockle shells
Used as short skis by the red-bearded Boat Person who arrives at Bankok. The shore people invite him to their water-ski regatta and joke that he should win the cockle-shell division.
Contemporary Trade Opportunities
A periodical monitored by Nero Altomare for commercial developments arising from the Boat People’s arrival. It publishes tips for trading with the “Fish People,” promotes the “Rapidity School of Languages,” and advertises “Fast Fish Special” courses. It “has taken the lead” in the new oceanic commerce.
Custodian of the keys
A former army buck sergeant who could unlock every door at the Pentagon. In the chapter 5 news roundup, he has become one of six newly appointed six-star generals issuing orders during the occupation.
Departures of Emperors
A “secret and huge door” in Domdaniel “used only for the ‘Departures of Emperors’ and never twice in the same century.” Carmody Overlark exits through it after his deposition.
Devil’s Head
An apparition that manifests in and around the Devil’s Well at the Dunes of Hell resort. “The Devil’s-Head effect was obtained. (It wasn’t an effect! it was real.)” It has one malevolent eye and a murderous voice. The narrator insists that the apparition is real.
Devil’s Well
A huge well at Emmet Briny’s resort that “bubbled up partly inside and partly outside the palace-like main building.” Connected by deep channels to both the Rio Grande and the Gulf of Mexico. Any kind of fish can be caught from it: “red snapper, sail fish, barracuda, channel catfish, ocean mackerel, octopus.” Robert Dolphin enters and exits through it.
Dolphin locks
An entrance mechanism in Domdaniel through which dolphins can enter the palace from the ocean. The little dolphin “descended into the palace by the ‘dolphin locks,’ and joined the intrigue.”
Domdaniel
The oceanic fortress-palace of the emperor of the World, located in the middle of the Ocean. Surrounded by the Nine Iron Walls. Contains the throne room with eschatological activators, the “Masterpieces of Death” gallery, the Jewelry Whale, multiple art galleries, and the notion mill. In literary tradition, a fabled undersea hall of evil spirits beneath Tunis, referenced in Southey’s Thalaba the Destroyer and Carlyle’s Sartor Resartus. Also called “the Holy Empire of Domdaniel.” Robert’s oceanic name is Wheecreoclessese.
Dragons-teeth soldiers
Carmody’s throne-room guards, with mechanical components and a name recalling the warriors sprung from sown dragon’s teeth in Greek myth. Revolutionaries kill most of them while Robert sits disguised on the throne. Robert reflects that replacements can be harvested from the field where the teeth were sown.
“Drowned Men’s Rag” composition
One of the pieces Oscar Judgement whistles while leading the underwater escape. The narrator says it “should be in the repertoire of every whistler” (chapter 7).
Dunes of Hell
Emmet Briny’s resort-hotel, restaurant, nightclub, and casino, located on barren land between Matamoros and the Rio Grande near the Gulf of Mexico. Meeting place of the Matamoros Chapter. Features the Devil’s Well, copper-gleaming kitchens, floor shows, and a gambling trade from both sides of the border. “It was natural desert there; and the big river, even when it flowed bank-full of water, did not alleviate the desert.”
Emperor of the World title
Carmody Overlark’s principal title. The emperor rules from Domdaniel with push-button access to plague, swarming, tidal waves, and the end of the world. His other titles include the Most Noble Tyrrhenian Tyrant, Slave and Mameluke emperor, and Master of Galleries. He is “getting about ready to abdicate.”
Exarch of Sharks title
One of Twenty-Three’s imperial titles, listed during his accession and again at the coronation.
“Fair Ghost Invisible, I Long for Thee” hymn
An old hymn Oscar Judgement whistles as he leads the humans up the invisible ninth-wall cliffs after Robert’s departure (chapter 7).
Fifth Reich
The political entity governing Cuxhaven in the novel’s world. Cuxhaven has “a regular army base, a regular naval shore-trooper base, a special army base, a commando base” under the Fifth Reich, implying a future German state beyond the historical Third Reich.
First Fish Night
The evening of June 13, the first night of commercial contact between the dry-land world and the Boat People. Marked by trading ferment, new-experience salons, the “Rapidity School of Languages,” and hit songs. “What was already popularly called ‘First Fish Night.’”
Fish People
Colloquial dry-land term for the Boat People and/or Sea People. “The Dry Landers have been calling the Boat People ‘Fish’ for the last half hour, and they say they know fish when they smell them.” Commercial reports use it for the new markets opened by the landings.
Fish-Faced Emperor title
The popular title of Twenty-Three after he becomes the 289th emperor. Robert’s seven-minute reign immediately precedes his.
Flying People
A people mentioned by Slackwater alongside the Sea People and the Land People. “The Flyers are shy about being seen, and we’re not.” Slackwater does not elaborate.
Fortune Wind
Emmet’s name for the burst of commercial opportunity that follows the Boat People’s arrival. “The Fortune Wind is blowing. It’s been blowing ever since the Boats came to shore.” Emmet Briny and others rush to mint gold coins and exploit trading opportunities before it subsides.
Friend of Sea People and Dolphins title
One of Twenty-Three’s formal titles, included in the declaration of his accession and the expanded title chain at the coronation.
Ghost Boats
An older name for the Boat People’s craft. “In calling their craft ‘Ghost Boats’ the old observers were more than half right.” The phrase implies that the boats, and perhaps the Boat People, have appeared before in history and been misidentified.
Golden Room
Domdaniel’s gallery of finely hammered gold statues representing creatures considered mythological on land. Twenty-Three conducts the tour in chapter 11. Some statues are translucent; others float in the electrically charged air. Casualties of factional fighting lie on the floor.
Green darkness
The luminous darkness of the underwater world. During the Committee’s first swim, living creatures and seaweeds give off light, and broken moonlight filters down from the surface (chapter 5).
Gules
The heraldic term for red. Twenty-Three imagines fallen secret agents with “their blood running out in rivulets of ‘gules’ or heraldic red” (chapter 14).
“Gyroscopic Whistle Boogie” composition
The tune Robert proposes as the signal for his invented pistol during the St. Malo’s episode. His enthusiasm for the invention helps turn his despair back toward activity (chapter 15).
Informal Committee
The Informal Committee for Advising Directors of Corporations and Nations. About one hundred chapters in thirty countries, loosely linked. Motto: “Save the World first of All. It will not matter if it is saved clumsily. And it will not matter if it is saved severally by each of a hundred different chapters. The world cannot have too much saving.”
Inter-Species Studies
Programs in which intelligent members of different species learn together. Robert attended Mexican and U.S. programs, including Harvard; Twenty-Three studied with him. Robert later names the Roy Mitsyas School of Interspecies Studies. His Harvard attendance was brief, and Twenty-Three says his verse got him failed out.
Inverness cape
Robert’s characteristic cloak, resembling an Inverness cape. During the escape, it conceals his artificial throat-gills. He retains it beneath several of his disguises.
Invisible People
The Sea People’s self-description. “We Sea People have always been the ‘Invisible People.’ We have been seen thousands and millions of times” but are consistently unrecognized. “Somebody will bump into me and then say ‘What in the world! I didn’t see you standing there!’” Their invisibility is a matter of perception: people on land fail to register them.
Islands of Bones
Remote burial grounds of the Sea People, compared to elephant graveyards. Carmody says that Breton fishermen knew about them before Columbus and that other accidental visitors were terrified by the ghosts. The Sea People’s habit of burying their dead helps account for the scarcity of their bodies on land.
Jewelry Whale
A hollowed sperm whale filled with jewels, sent to the new emperor by Emperor Shaktu of Djauhar-Laut. A decoy containing imitation jewels and hidden jewel thieves is mistakenly barbecued. The genuine whale reaches the Grand Piscina, missing the ten thousand small pieces taken by Carmody and Margret.
Karachi Kartel
A group of six hundred jewel thieves expected at the coronation. Three hide in the decoy Jewelry Whale and are baked with it. The little dolphin later reports that hundreds of thieves have been captured.
Karachi Men
In the Domdaniel court, a culinary term: persons from Karachi served as food. The Seneschal of the Kitchens identifies the banquet dish: “It’s man, Karachi Man. . . . How could anyone ever forget that taste?” Lafferty presents the cannibalism with the deadpan he uses for the killings at Calcutta.
Lightning and Iron
The military defense system at Cuxhaven under the Fifth Reich: “It had ‘Lightning and Iron’ defenses both interior and exterior.” The forces “began to shake and tremble when they came within the influence of the corporate aura of the Boat People.” Modern defenses prove useless against the Boat People’s psychological force.
Limited Edition Lighter
A special lighter “of which there are only eight in the world,” carried by Robert Dolphin as part of his secret agent persona. He uses it to light his Sullivan cigarettes with studied nonchalance. The little dolphin eventually steals it.
Masterpieces of Death
A twelve-sided room in Domdaniel with over three thousand paintings and living-color sculptures of famous deaths, all of which occurred in this room. Also called the “Deaths of Famous Persons” gallery. Carmody serves as docent. “These vivid deaths (but vivid means alive, these alive deaths).” Carmody presents the works as records of deaths that occurred before the artists.
Matamoros Chapter
The central group of the Informal Committee for Advising Directors of Corporations and Nations. Its thirteen members come from Mexico, the United States, the Catalonian Republic, Sierra Leone, Italy, Ireland, Turkey, Norway, San Simeon, and the Jolly Rogers Floating Realm of Practicing Pirates. They meet at Emmet Briny’s Dunes of Hell resort.
Mile Down Ride-In Cinema
An underwater cinema where films assembled from hidden-camera footage of Robert’s adventures are shown. Slackwater recommends it to Oldapple in chapter 13.
Morphic
A being able to change its own form. Carmody becomes a convincing Robert Dolphin, then changes back without removing a material disguise. Robert, by contrast, uses a costume to impersonate Carmody.
“Nile Down Meander” composition
A ragtime piece Oscar Judgement whistles during the underwater escape. The party’s throat-gills permit them to sing and whistle together (chapter 7).
Nine Iron Walls
The concentric defenses of Domdaniel. Robert guides the first Committee party through eight underwater walls. The ninth consists of invisible cliffs rising above the sea. The humans climb them after surfacing, but a delivery pilot reveals that they were already outside the fortress.
Noble Nabob Twenty-Three title
An honorific for the Tyrrhenian Fish who becomes emperor. Robert returns the rare lighter to him during the confrontation over the throne (chapter 14).
Notion mill
The mill in Domdaniel that once ground salt for the oceans and now, a Boat Person says, grinds out notions for the world. Its products are contrasted with rational thought. The account leaves open whether it supplied the impulse for the latest swarming.
Novelty enjoyment spans
The Boat People’s taste for elaborate things that vanish as soon as they tire of them. Contemporary Trade Opportunities encourages manufacturers to supply this market; the periodical evaporates explosively when the next edition is due (chapter 5).
Ocean Rumor
An oceanic news service also available on land. A Boat Person calls it “like Siemens Reports, only farther-reaching and more accurate.” It circulates news of Robert’s despair and runs seventeen minutes behind during the coronation.
Oceanic Graphics Room
A gallery visited under Twenty-Three’s guidance in chapter 11. Its clear water and lighting make the pictures almost indistinguishable from the windows overlooking Domdaniel’s submarine gardens.
Old Dory Song Book
The source named for the four-line oceanic verse in the chapter 11 epigraphs, beginning “The hills of Ocean skip like lambs.”
Olympus
The mythic home of the Greek gods becomes a name for the novel’s privileged “high life.” After leaving Domdaniel, Carmody and Margret occupy an invisible fragment of Olympus above the coronation banquet. Margret says its ledges can be placed wherever they wish because Olympus is a state of mind.
Palace Cruelty
The conspicuous violence expected on a new ruler’s takeover day. Twenty-Three says that he and his party will have to observe the tradition if they win.
Palace revolution
Twenty-Three’s plan to depose Carmody. It involves an armed uprising and a substitute emperor who can publicly abdicate in Carmody’s place. Robert supplies the disguise, switches off the plague, and briefly reigns himself before Twenty-Three tricks him out of the throne.
Phooo
Robert first calls his projected work Phooo in chapter 4, then Phooo, or The Memoirs of a Secret Agent in chapter 6. Twenty-Three calls it Phoco, or The Annals of a Secret Agent in chapter 14. Robert writes on a whistle-activated typewriter at about a thousand words a minute. The final exchange identifies the novel with his completed memoirs and annals: “Did he really complete that astonishing body of work? Sure he did. This is it.”
Poor Fishes’ Broadcast
Robert’s verse broadcasts over the Ocean Network during chapter 11. They proclaim his imminent arrival, irritating Twenty-Three, who needs him back in Domdaniel for the revolution.
Princes of the Extreme Depths
Deep-ocean beings among the coronation guests, so strange that Robert can hardly comprehend their forms. As a surface-dwelling dolphin, he feels kinship with the upper tenth of oceanic life rather than these creatures of the lower depths (chapter 16).
Raffles
A. J. Raffles, the fictional gentleman-thief created by E. W. Hornung (1899). Robert’s Sullivan cigarettes evoke “the palmy days of Raffles” and “the great gentleman Crook Raffles.” The allusion associates Robert’s secret-agent persona with Hornung’s gentleman criminal.
Remorse pistol
Dan Asal’s gun at St. Malo’s. It returns when discarded and reloads when emptied, reminding him of the killing for which he is still only partly repentant. Robert’s attempts to use it on himself fail.
Ring of the Fisherman
The papal Anulus Piscatoris, revealed in the Slackwater interview to be properly a Sea Person’s emblem. “The non-oceanic Popes who also wear ‘The Ring of the Fisherman’ do it only by special grace and dispensation.” Slackwater says that the Ring “always identifies a Sea Person,” taking the fisherman of the papal title literally.
Schism Room of Subliminal Art
A Domdaniel gallery in which each painting and sculpture is unevenly divided into two pieces. Twenty-Three describes it but turns the party toward the Beauty Room instead (chapter 11).
Sea Cattle
Carmody’s term for the Boat People. He says that they are easily produced and sent swarming when their numbers become too great or when he wishes. The livestock metaphor presents them as a population bred and deployed by the rulers of the depths.
Sea Haven / Ocean Eden
Alternate names for the oceanic realm or for Carmody’s palace. “I spend almost all of my time here in ‘Sea Haven’ as some of my associates call it. Others call it ‘Ocean Eden.’ There are dozens of other names for my place.” Also used for the “Third White House,” the president’s secret oceanic retreat.
Sea People
The underwater human populations of which the Boat People are the swarming contingent. They can breathe air and water and often live unnoticed on land. Slackwater describes their government, customs, arts, and relations with the emperor in chapter 13.
Second Fish Day
The day after First Fish Night, when Robert Dolphin makes his trip to World Medical Headquarters in New York to warn about Boat People Disease. “It was still quite early in the morning of ‘Second Fish Day.’” The names (“First Fish Night,” “Second Fish Day”) count time from the Boat People’s arrival.
Secret Agent One-Plus title
Carmody’s secret-agent identity at the coronation. Its rank exceeds Robert’s Number One. Carmody reads Robert’s thoughts and, with Margret, takes ten thousand jewels from the genuine Jewelry Whale.
Seneschal of the Kitchens title
A court functionary in Domdaniel responsible for preparing meals, including the Karachi Men baked in the Jewelry Whale. “It’s man, Karachi Man,” the Seneschal explains. He regrets that “one of the three has disappeared.”
Seneschal of the Whale title
A court functionary in charge of the Jewelry Whale. His plan involves disguising himself as one of the towing dolphins and maneuvering the whale during the coronation. Part of the elaborate Domdaniel court hierarchy.
Sharp-memory thorns
The large “sharp-memory” thorns on Robert’s roses for Margret. The card distinguishes their lasting hurt from the flowers’ fading beauty: “Roses fade and die, but thorns are forever.”
Siemens Reports
A news and intelligence service followed by the Matamoros Chapter. Its reports cover the landings, the occupations, commercial developments, synthetic throat-gills, and rumors of the plague. Esther carries a miniature receiver.
Spirits of the Deep
Robert Dolphin’s code name for the oceanic powers he serves. “You people I call them by the code name ‘Spirits of the Deep.’ Or else I call them nothing at all.” Also used by Carmody: the Boat People are “the ‘Sea Cattle’ of the ‘Spirits of the Deep.’”
St. Malo’s
A purgatorial settlement in the ocean where Robert Dolphin finds himself during his “Dark Night of Despond.” Its “splintered shore” is inhabited by purgatorial persons, including Dan Asal. The name recalls Saint-Malo, the Breton port.
Sullivan cigarettes
Robert Dolphin’s signature prop. He has a novelty printer make Sullivan logos for ordinary cigarettes. The brand recalls the gentleman thief A. J. Raffles. Twenty-Three produces a genuine Sullivan in chapter 14, says that the surviving stock belongs to royalty, and uses it to lure Robert from the throne. The little dolphin later displays her own mastery of this secret-agent performance.
Swarming
The Boat People’s mode of mass deployment: arriving simultaneously at every port in coordinated waves. Controlled by the throne activators in Domdaniel (“Turn swarm on,” “Turn swarm off”). Robert pushes “Turn Swarm Off” during the palace revolution. The term likens the Boat People to swarming bees or locusts.
“Terror in the Deep” composition
A piece Oscar Judgement whistles while leading the escape through Domdaniel’s underwater defenses. Despite its title, his rendition is “almost merry” (chapter 7).
Throne activators
Buttons and inscriptions on the throne of Domdaniel control swarming, plague, and other world-shaping events. Some inscriptions are set in jewels, and others are in Carmody’s fine handwriting. Robert presses “Turn Swarm Off” and “Turn Plague Off” during the revolution.
Throne birds
Margret’s disparaging name for the land rulers whom Carmody used to hypnotize and advise: presidents, prime ministers, premiers, commissars, and kings (chapter 6).
“Through the Sky, My Hearties” song
The old ballooners’ song sung and whistled by the escape party as they climb toward the ocean’s surface. Its full title is “Through the Sky, My Hearties, Through the Sky We’ll Climb” (chapter 7).
Tyrrhenian
A name associated historically with the Etruscans and used in the novel for related oceanic peoples and fish. Twenty-Three distinguishes Tyrrhenian Fish from Tyrrhenian Men while claiming common descent from Carmody. The term occurs in many of his imperial titles.
Unfathomable Secret Look
The signature facial expression of a secret agent. “She did the Secret Agent look, sometimes called the ‘Unfathomable Secret Look’ excellently.” The little dolphin masters it; Robert performs it routinely. The look belongs to the novel’s running treatment of espionage as performance.
Water-walking
A skill practiced by some Boat People “who are required to serve a term of duty as water-walkers and wise men who know all the languages.” They do it “wherever we believe the people are simple-minded and would be impressed by it.” The water near port is “worse than walking on plowed ground. It’s worse than walking through a jungle.”
Wheecreoclessese
The name Robert uses among the Spirits of the Deep and the inhabitants of Domdaniel. He describes it as “spoken with rather a whistling tone” (chapter 3).
“When the Sea Gives Up Its Dead” composition
A piece Oscar Judgement whistles underwater, with extra harmonics from the throat-gills. Its title recalls Revelation 20:13 (chapter 7).
Whistle-activated typewriter
Robert Dolphin’s writing instrument: “This machine will type as fast as I can talk, and it does not make mistakes. I do not have to touch the machine, only talk-whistle at it. It works underwater too.” Robert composes Phooo on it, and the final lines identify his completed memoirs with the novel.
Wildest Rumors of the Morning
A ranking reported by Siemens in chapter 8. Fifth place belongs to the rumor that Boat People Disease will kill ninety percent of humanity unless an antidote is found by sundown. More than 130,000 almost identical calls have reached rumor central.
